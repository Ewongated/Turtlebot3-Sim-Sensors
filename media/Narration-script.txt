0:00–0:20 — Task and pipeline
This project demonstrates visual waypoint navigation with a TurtleBot3 in MuJoCo.
The robot must navigate around obstacles using LiDAR, then dock at an ArUco-marked
station. Our integrated pipeline combines sensing, an extended Kalman filter,
and feedback control using the estimated state.

0:20–0:45 — Part A: model
The state is planar position and heading. Differential-drive kinematics relate
left and right wheel movement to forward movement and rotation. We use measured
encoder increments rather than assuming wheel commands equal actual motion.
The moving-state linearisation is controllable; its local rank drops at rest.
Marker orientation provides heading information in addition to range and bearing.

0:45–1:05 — Part B: sensing
Camera poses use the known marker and camera/base frames. We fitted intrinsics
from eighteen rendered checkerboard views, then checked six held-out views.
Focal lengths were within 0.42 percent of the configuration, with 0.064-pixel
mean held-out reprojection error. This is simulation calibration, not hardware
calibration.

1:05–1:30 — Part C: estimation
The EKF predicts pose and covariance from the encoders. Camera observations then
correct accumulated drift when the marker is detected and the innovation passes
the outlier gate. During dropout, prediction continues and uncertainty grows.
Q describes motion uncertainty and R measurement uncertainty. This configuration
uses covariance multipliers of ten for Q and one for R.

1:30–2:08 — Nominal run, uninterrupted at normal speed
Here is the complete nominal run. The overview is on the left and the actual
controller camera image is on the right. Below are true and estimated pose,
with estimation error and three-sigma uncertainty bands. The controller receives
the EKF estimate, while truth is used for evaluation. Watch the uncertainty grow
when the marker leaves view, then shrink after camera correction. The robot
approaches the station and confirms arrival through accepted camera observations.

2:08–2:28 — Nominal result
The nominal trial completed in 37.3 seconds without a recorded collision.
Whole-run position RMSE was 7.12 centimetres. Final true lateral error was
2.25 centimetres and heading error was 3.30 degrees. Missing camera frames
cannot add evidence towards the five confirmations required for arrival.

2:28–3:14 — Noisy-camera trial, uninterrupted at normal speed
The second trial adds pixel-noise standard deviation five and thirty percent
random camera-observation dropout. The robot still predicts from encoders between
corrections. An image appearing on screen does not mean an observation was used:
the update indicator shows whether the EKF actually accepted it. Near docking,
translation is disabled when the marker is lost, and the robot rotates to
reacquire it. This complete disturbed trial succeeds in 45.3 seconds without
a recorded collision. It tests one disturbance setting, not every possible failure.

3:14–3:44 — Part D: feedback and actuators
The LQR tracker uses estimated cross-track and heading errors against a latched
reference path. Its gain comes from the stated path-error model and effort weights.
A feedforward actuator compensator accounts for identified wheel friction and gain.
Docking keeps the original mapping. A wider rotation threshold reduced repeated
switching in the slow obstacle case. Saturation and large angles limit what the
linear analysis alone can guarantee.

3:44–4:14 — Part E: repeated trials
We checked two seeds in each of four conditions: nominal, camera noise and
dropout, shifted obstacle with encoder bias, and reduced floor friction.
All eight completed without recorded collisions or timeouts. Mean per-trial
position RMSE was 7.54 centimetres. These online navigation trials are separate
from the earlier estimator replays and historical controller comparison.

4:14–4:38 — Tradeoff and limitations
Changing rotation entry from 0.4 to 0.8 radians reduced the slow case from
90.3 to 36.9 seconds. However, mean estimation RMSE increased from 6.20 to
7.54 centimetres. This is a speed–accuracy tradeoff. The earlier 4.90-centimetre
result used a different sensor and controller configuration, so it is not the
result of this integrated version.

4:38–5:00 — Provenance and conclusion
The repository contains the code, run commands, seeds, traces, source snapshots
and plots. This is simulation evidence from a small development sample.
Raw marker measurements retain range bias and occasional large heading errors.
Historical studies without supporting summaries are excluded from headline results.
